The Backstory

How the family came to be.

A street kid, a wandering monk, a half-orc looking for his ancestors, and two children who’d never been protected before.

I. Alone in Vladrich

Alestur doesn’t remember the faces of his parents, but he remembers the alleys. By the time he was old enough to count years, Vladrich had already taught him every lesson he needed: trust no one, owe no one, and never look surprised when the world picks your pocket. He survived. That was the whole of it.

Years later, in Elara’s Capitol, he saw a high elf begging on the steps of the chapel. She wasn’t crying. She was just thin, and quiet, and very still. He didn’t know why he stopped — he tells himself, even now, that he doesn’t know.

“You stop because somebody has to. That’s the only reason anyone ever stops.”

II

II. Two travelers

Alexandria Willthorne had been wandering since her parents were killed in a feud she’d never been told about. She’d learned to read people the way Alestur had learned to read shadows — out of necessity, and quickly. When patrolmen began harassing her for sport, she didn’t fight back; she calculated.

Alestur did the fighting for her. Badly. Loudly. And then he sat beside her on the steps, checking the cut on his lip, and said something dry enough that she actually laughed. They travelled together after that, for no reason either of them could articulate.

III

III. An arrow at the right moment

Grogth found them, or they found him, at an Elarian outpost on the edge of Wustoria. He was already mid-rescue — two children pulled from under a collapsing wagon, a guard closing in on his back. The arrow that dropped the guard came from a rooftop. Alestur landed beside him a moment later, Alexandria already moving to the children.

Grogth had spent his life trying to honor his ancestors the way his clan demanded: through conquest. Standing in the dust of that outpost, watching two strangers do what his clan never would have, he understood for the first time that there might be another way.

IV

IV. The siblings

The children were Wilks and Seriah Adams. Wilks had spent his entire short life convinced no one would ever help them; Seriah, younger, had taken his word for it. The first night, neither of them slept — they kept watching the three strangers across the fire, waiting for the trick.

The trick never came. By the second week, Seriah was riding on Grogth’s shoulders. By the second month, Wilks had stopped flinching when Alexandria reached toward him. None of them said the word family out loud. They didn’t have to.

“We never said it. We just stopped leaving.”
